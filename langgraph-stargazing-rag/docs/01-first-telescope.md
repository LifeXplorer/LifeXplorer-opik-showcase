# Choosing and Setting Up Your First Telescope

## Aperture and mount

Members of the Brightwater Stargazers' Club are told the same thing: buy aperture, but only as much as you will carry. The club loaner pool holds a 130 mm Dobsonian (called "Pennywhistle"), a 200 mm Dobsonian (called "Old Faithful") and an 80 mm refractor on a small equatorial mount. New members should start with Pennywhistle, which fits in a hatchback and needs no tools to assemble.

## Collimation

A Newtonian-style telescope must be collimated before each session. The club uses a Cheshire eyepiece and a laser collimator, and checks the primary mirror's centre mark first. If the dot of the secondary mirror is not centred in the Cheshire, turn the three secondary tilt screws by a quarter turn at a time. A full collimation takes about ten minutes once you have done it three times.

## Cool-down time

The mirror must reach air temperature or images shimmer. Allow 30 minutes for the 130 mm Dobsonian and 60 minutes for the 200 mm Old Faithful. Leave the dust cap off and the telescope in the shade of the observing field's wind fence while it cools.

## Finder alignment

Align the red-dot finder on a distant chimney or streetlight in daylight, never on the Sun. Centre the target in the main eyepiece first, then adjust the finder's two thumbscrews until the red dot sits on the same target.
