# Observing Conditions and Planning

## Seeing and transparency

Seeing describes how steady the air is, rated by the club on the Pickering scale of 1 to 10, where 7 or higher is good enough for planetary detail. Transparency describes how clear the sky is and decides how faint an object you can see. A night can have excellent transparency and poor seeing, so check both before a session.

## Dark-sky limits

The club's Marrow Ridge site has a Bortle rating of 3. The car park at the Brightwater community centre is a Bortle 6 and is only used for lunar and planetary sessions. At Bortle 6 the Milky Way is not visible to the eye, so schedule deep-sky targets for Marrow Ridge.

## Dark adaptation

Allow 25 minutes for full dark adaptation. Use only red torches with the brightness turned low, and never look at a phone screen unless it is set to the club's red-screen mode. One white flash resets the clock.

## Moon phase planning

Deep-sky sessions are scheduled within four days either side of the new moon. Sessions near full moon are reserved for lunar observing, and the club log records the terminator crater of the night.
