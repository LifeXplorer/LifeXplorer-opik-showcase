# Astrophotography Basics

## Starting with a phone

Smartphone astrophotography works best on the Moon. Hold the phone against the eyepiece, or use the club's 3D-printed eyepiece clamp, and shoot short video clips instead of single photos. Stack the best frames afterwards with free software.

## Camera settings for the Milky Way

For a wide-field Milky Way shot on a fixed tripod, the club recommends a 20-second exposure at ISO 3200 with the lens fully open. This is the 500 rule in practice: divide 500 by your focal length to find the longest exposure before stars trail.

## Tracking and guiding

Exposures longer than 30 seconds need a tracking mount. The club's Sky-Tracker pool has two portable trackers, which can be booked for one night at a time. Polar alignment accuracy decides how long you can expose; aim for under five arcminutes of error.

## Calibration frames

Take 20 dark frames and 20 flat frames for every imaging session, using the same camera temperature and focus as the lights. Flats correct for dust and vignetting, and darks remove hot pixels. Skip bias frames if you only stack with the club's pipeline.
