# Mashing and grain at Copperkettle

## What mash temperature should I use for a dry, crisp pale ale?
Mash at 148-150 F (64-66 C) for 60 minutes. Lower temperatures favor beta-amylase,
which produces highly fermentable sugars and a drier, crisper finish. A mash at
156 F (69 C) leaves more dextrins and gives a fuller, sweeter body.

## How much water do I need for a 5-gallon batch?
Plan on 1.25 quarts of strike water per pound of grain, then sparge with enough
170 F (77 C) water to collect about 6.5 gallons of wort. About a gallon is lost
to boil-off and a little more to trub, so you finish with roughly 5 gallons in the fermenter.

## Why is my mash pH important?
Aim for a mash pH between 5.2 and 5.6, measured at room temperature. Below 5.2
the wort can taste thin and sour. Above 5.6 you extract tannins from the husks,
which taste harsh and astringent. Dark roasted grains push pH down; pale
malts with very soft water push it up.

## Can I use crystal malt in a Copperkettle house lager?
Yes, but keep it under 5 percent of the grist. The house lager recipe (CK-Lager-1)
uses 2 percent Crystal 20L for color and a trace of caramel sweetness.
