# Yeast and fermentation at Copperkettle

## What temperature should I ferment an ale at?
Hold ale yeast at 64-68 F (18-20 C) for the first three days, then let it rise
to 70 F (21 C) to finish. Fermenting hotter than 72 F (22 C) produces fusel
alcohols and a solvent-like taste. Keep the fermenter in the cellar corner
marked "ale zone", where the temperature stays steady.

## How long should the primary fermentation last?
Most ales finish in 7 to 10 days. Do not go by airlock bubbles; take two
hydrometer readings 48 hours apart. If the gravity has not moved, fermentation
is done. Give strong beers over 7 percent ABV two full weeks.

## Do I need to make a yeast starter?
For ales under 1.060 original gravity, a single fresh liquid yeast pack is
enough. For lagers, or any beer above 1.060, make a 1.5 liter starter with
dried malt extract 24 hours ahead. Dry yeast needs no starter; sprinkle it
straight onto the cooled wort.

## What are the signs of a stuck fermentation?
The gravity stays above 1.020 after ten days and the beer tastes sweet. First
raise the temperature by 3 F and gently rouse the yeast. If nothing moves after
two days, pitch a packet of rehydrated dry yeast such as a neutral ale strain.
